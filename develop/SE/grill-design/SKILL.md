# grill-design

Read context first; answer what the evidence already settles. Ask only the remaining questions, in one batch of at most six short items:
1. What problem and outcome matter now?
2. What is the simplest solution that works today?
3. Which real requirement forces more complexity?
4. Which assumption is least supported?
5. What will be hardest to change later?
6. What fails first if the design is wrong?

Lite mode: at most four questions covering goal, simplest approach, failure, and proof. Let the user answer together; do not drip-feed interrogations. Follow up only on a material unresolved decision. For personal-design, allow exploration and label future-only choices; do not turn them into delivery requirements. Asking is not authorization to implement.
